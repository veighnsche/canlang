## Original policy versus the authored policy

| Concern | Original behavior | Authored composition proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expense reviewer | One assigned `Expense.reviewer`, distinct from claimant, with current reviewer role and `can_work` at the location. | Retain that locked primary and nominate one distinct eligible secondary. Both must approve the same request; claimant cannot vote. Only the assigned primary still records the terminal `Decision`. |
| Creative reviewer | The current owner-manager validates and publishes an immutable `Revision`; no human request/vote/quorum protocol exists. | Owner-manager requests review by two distinct other current managers. Both must approve; neither gains publication authority. Owner-manager still publishes. |
| Frozen evidence | Expense submission locks business fields and requires matching receipt transcription. Creative validation creates an immutable graph/mapping/output/duration revision associated with its validation delivery. | Add locked typed request snapshots and immutable votes. Expense freezes claimant, primary, secondary, location, authorization, purpose, category, amount/currency, date, receipt and a matching transcription, plus submission and admitted subject version. Creative freezes owner, nominees, revision number/version, graph and all four mappings, limits, associated validation identity and successful valid digest. |
| Quorum and time | Neither original Expense nor Creative has this two-vote quorum or review deadline. | Exactly two distinct affirmative votes from the named roster, with an explicit required future `expires` input. No default review duration or automatic renewal. Approval on/after the deadline refuses. |
| Rejection | Expense primary can reject with a nonblank reason. Creative validation failure prevents publication. | A named eligible reviewer can veto an open round with a nonblank reason. Expense primary records the separate rejected `Decision`; Creative veto leaves publication/current pointer unchanged. A prior affirmative voter cannot submit another vote or change it into a veto. |
| Recovery before finalization | Expense claimant can withdraw only a submitted claim with unavailable primary, then make one linked correction draft. Creative can validate a new immutable revision. | Explicit requester escalation and supersession close a round, retain its evidence and create a fresh ID/round with zero votes. Expense keeps the original primary and replaces only the secondary; primary loss uses original withdrawal/correction. Creative nominates a complete fresh roster. Rejected rounds cannot be superseded in these sources: Expense requires rejection/correction; Creative requires a new revision and request. |
| Recovery after Expense decision | Original Expense has no approved-to-withdrawn recovery. | **Additional domain policy proposal:** finance may `invalidate_unpaid` after deadline expiry or roster eligibility loss, only with exact review/approved Decision evidence, a nonblank reason and zero reimbursements. It sets the claim withdrawn, preserving Decision and votes. Claimant then creates a correction draft with fresh transcription/request/votes. This is not a reassignment or payment override. |
| Final business effect | Expense reimbursement is separately authorized finance bookkeeping of external payment evidence. Creative publication discloses the published graph to current members while its template is active. | Recheck the current review at finalization, retain the original finance/publication guards and execution order, and never turn quorum into a role or an external payment API. Already reimbursed claims cannot be invalidated. Publication is not retroactively undone by review expiry. |

These are explicit pilot policies, not inferred language defaults or proof that every company should use two reviewers. Current role/work eligibility is an operation-time condition; historical votes and Decisions are immutable facts, not stored invariants that become invalid when a reviewer later leaves. The source's `by=members`/role predicates rely on the defining current-context producer; fixture roles alone do not establish live team revocation.

CanApprove demonstrates a different complete outcome. It owns `Document`, immutable `Submission` file/note/due revisions, one mutable pending assignment, a scoped coordinator, revision/assignment-bound `Due` occurrences, and attributed request/decision/reminder `Notice` records with selected mail-delivery associations. New submissions withdraw pending versions and retain finalized evidence. Coordinator reassignment increments assignment, keeps the file/note/due and replaces the timer at `max(now,due)`; overdue status does not make the review expire. Decision changes the exact pending submission, cancels its timer, then creates its decision notice. Committed notice dispatch reloads the selected record and rechecks current pending revision/assignment/reviewer eligibility; delivery failure cannot rewrite a business decision. The two compositions do not move, remove or claim to implement those document outcomes, mail dispatch or scheduling. A reusable abstraction must accommodate their differences rather than treating CanApprove's due date as the new deadline or inventing notice/timer behavior in these pilots.

## Complete workflows, authority and evaluation order

Expense retains its owning Employee/Location imports, draft CRUD, viewer-authorized reviewer choices, receipt transcription, submit, primary decision, unavailable-primary withdrawal, one correction draft, finance reimbursement, historical source retention/matching/access mapping, locks/invariants, original presentation and inline examples. Historical imports remain evidence, never reconstructed live approvals/payments. Equal employee names retain distinct user identities; descriptive work roles grant no reviewer authority and HR private fields receive no new grant.

The added Expense flow is submit → request snapshot → two immutable votes → primary Decision → finance reimbursement. Request selects one matching transcription in stable ID order before creating the snapshot. Each approval creates its vote before counting votes, then sets request state even for the first vote (`open`), so the request is intended to supply a version fence shared by concurrent reviewers. Rejection creates its negative vote before setting rejected. Supersession marks the old request superseded before creating the next round. Decision loads the latest round, checks exact evidence and admitted subject version, creates immutable `Decision` evidence with request/version linkage, then changes claim status. Reimbursement rechecks latest exact approved unexpired review/current roster and its matching approved Decision, creates reimbursement evidence, then sets reimbursed. It deliberately compares the Decision's recorded subject version with the request, rather than equating the request version to the claim version after the Decision has changed status.

Creative retains chat composition, file policy including JSON graphs, editable owned templates, inspection, immutable validation revisions, publication, budgets, image generation, chat-turn generation, targeted stop, reconciliation, skipped-job release, membership/conversation cancellation handlers, associated progress/accounting/output finalization, all four original pages and their examples. Its added review operates on `Revision`, not an invented `WorkflowRevision` model. Draft Template edits clear inspection but cannot rewrite the previously frozen Revision being reviewed. Review does not reserve an image job or submit a provider generation. Original validation creates the revision, sends safe validation, then associates its delivery. Publication loads the latest review and checks exact revision/version, quorum, deadline and current roster before setting `Revision.published/digest`, then `Template.current`. Ordinary generation still freezes the selected published revision, reserves allowance/concurrency before sending and associates the submission afterwards; unknown usage keeps its hold, terminal execution releases concurrency, and finalized outputs remain position-unique.

For both domains, `current_review` selects the greatest round under the exact typed parent; `exact_review` compares business evidence, and approval requires the two nominated affirmative votes. Unique per-parent round and per-request voter declarations and locks are authored. Exported Request/Vote models and operations confer no record authority. These mutations require one same-owner staged transaction with current admission, expected-version/uniqueness checks, fresh dependency revalidation and rollback on any late failure. In particular, the first approval's same-state request write must produce the intended request fence; concurrent voting/supersession/finalization cannot rely on UI serialization. Canonical receipt lookup/replay must precede stale versions and reevaluation of defaults/business effects. An identical operation-ID replay reuses its outcome; another invocation attempting a duplicate vote refuses.

The existing Expense/ReceiptExtract grants and Creative unpublished Template/Revision grants remain unchanged. New Expense review rows/votes are readable by the requester, nominated currently work-eligible reviewers and scoped finance; the secondary obtains the frozen review evidence, not general claim/employee access. Creative review rows/votes are readable only by current managers who are the requester or named nominees; ordinary members receive no unpublished review graph/validation grant. Those row predicates, embedded validation leaves, parent navigation and file attachment/download authority still need the owning executable grant joins. Read operations return viewer-authorized request collections; exporting a read scenario does not switch it to authority-mode enumeration.
