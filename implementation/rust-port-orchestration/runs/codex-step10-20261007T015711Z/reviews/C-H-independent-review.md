## Findings and disposition

Two concrete findings were independently reproduced and corrected by root. No unresolved product finding remains in the reviewed finite scope after those corrections.

1. **CSV invalid partial inputs suppressed a later valid candidate.** For required string `customer` and optional boolean `urgent`, `customer,urgent\nc-1,yes\nc-1,` produced `invalid,duplicate`, with the duplicate pointing at invalid row 0 and `candidate_count:0`. Selecting row 1 then returned a duplicate outcome and invoked nothing. The cause was recording invalid partial mapped inputs in `seen`, then consulting that map for duplicate admission. Read-only `git show 2959081:packages/interfaces/src/http/csv.ts` confirms the same logic at the frozen checkpoint; this was a preexisting consumer defect, not a shared-grammar regression. Root now consults only `firstValid` in `reviewCsvCandidates`, so invalid rows cannot establish duplicate identity. Original negative evidence remains in `consumer-review/probe-results.json` and `csv-preexisting-source.txt`.

2. **Redirect overflow was relabeled timeout during slow reader cancellation.** A 302 with Location, nine body bytes against cap eight, a 10 ms deadline, and cancellation completing after 35 ms produced `HttpTransportError(timeout)` in text, binary, and streaming readers. Overflow had already been detected. Root moved `HttpBodyLimitError` ahead of deadline/caller mapping in the redirect-drain catch, matching the final bounded readers. Original negative evidence remains in `consumer-review/slow-cancel-before.json`.

Independent renewed Node **v22.0.0** probes against the corrected private emitted consumers passed. Three CSV rows now produce `invalid,valid,duplicate`, with row 2 referring to valid row 1; candidate count is one. A separately constructed canonical JSON SHA-256 gives `532a510fcc6aa867d13b00c475a78e4af203a83a92b76a0b2ff5d372c193a96a`, matching the review. Selecting rows 1 and 2 commits exactly once and preserves the duplicate outcome. All three slow-cancel HTTP probes now produce `HttpBodyLimitError` with status 302/cap 8, send no second request, and release the body lock. Raw result: `consumer-review/probe-after-results.json`; reproducible source: `probe-after.mjs`. The successful CSV invocation uses an explicit scripted invoker, while parsing, identity, CSRF, review, consent, and commit execute through the real handler.
