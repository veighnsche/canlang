# CanLang adoption evaluation task list

## Objective

Evaluate whether the proposed CanLang product is designed to make it economical for a company to replace selected SaaS subscriptions with AI-generated applications tailored to its own operations. The intended company owns the application and its business rules. Its staff would use the browser or an authorized chatbot; they are not expected to author Can or maintain a conventional application stack.

The primary outcome is adoption. Token efficiency, reliable generation and editing, adequate interfaces, trustworthy behavior, operating cost and maintainability contribute to that outcome. A shorter source file is useful only when it preserves the required behavior. A feature-rich language is useful only when its complexity earns its place.

User clarification, 2026-10-04: one large SaaS subscription may be replaced by several smaller, composed Can apps. Evaluate the complete replacement of the company's required workflows, including cross-app work, rather than treating each app as an independent subscription saving or requiring parity with unused suite features. Include shared records and identity, permission continuity, navigation, reporting, migration, coordinated changes and recovery, integration upkeep, and any subscriptions the company must retain. Smaller business modules do not inherently require separate deployments. Composition's benefits and costs both need evidence; this clarification extends the evaluation framing without changing the captured source baseline.

This is an evaluation plan, not a decision to add features, rewrite the drafts or implement tooling. The existing design is reviewable. The evaluation must challenge assistant-created restrictions as well as proposed extensions. “Perfect” means pursuing the strongest defensible design for this audience; the audit cannot prove universal fitness or predict adoption from source inspection alone.

## Draft-only product evaluation

The evidence is `.can` design drafts, their `.md` requirements, and **handwritten `.mjs` examples of desired output**. The MJS files are not generated and do not demonstrate translation correctness. There is no implemented Can compiler, standard library or infrastructure to evaluate. Any references below to generation, deployment, rendering or runtime behavior describe the proposed product or bounded draft-authoring experiments, not existing Can execution capabilities.

The decision is whether the product design serves the intended companies, what should improve, and which assumptions need later validation. This is not a purchase-today, deployment-readiness or release evaluation. **Missing implementation must not be scored as a design defect, counted as an adoption blocker, or used as grounds to abandon Can.** Record that stage boundary once; mark observations requiring execution as unverified without multiplying the same absence into failures across the checklist.

Real design problems remain reviewable: conflicting contracts, missing required business behavior, unexplained dependencies, or evidenced feasibility/cost problems. Distinguish these from the fact that a coherent contract has not yet been implemented. Assess prospective value and costs conditionally, with explicit assumptions; do not assume unresolved semantics work perfectly or report predicted outcomes as measurements. Apply this boundary to all reports, experiments, JEV requests and the final verdict.

## Fair comparison rules

The comparison unit is a company's required business outcomes under stated conditions. It is not an app count, a vendor's entire feature catalogue or source length alone. Record the relevant conditions in the existing journey/experiment evidence before judging alternatives: users, workflows, data scale, quality and access requirements, starting situation, time horizon and available support. These rules apply to all three owners and to JEV inputs; they do not add checklist items or prescribe a winner.

| Potentially biased comparison | Required comparison |
| --- | --- |
| One Can app against an entire suite; or several independent apps credited as a complete replacement | Compare the composed Can solution with the company's required suite workflows, including cross-app behavior. Unused features are not mandatory parity; missing required behavior cannot be omitted to make Can look smaller. |
| Can against an enterprise SaaS vendor's needs, or only against handpicked trivial CRUD cases | Use the selected company's actual scale, security, reliability, accessibility and organizational needs. Sample ordinary work and important exceptions. Separate audience coverage from correctness within that audience; unsupported use cases establish scope limits, not automatic failure of every supported case. Do not drop a required journey after seeing an unfavorable result. |
| Can's compact imports against handwritten conventional infrastructure; or every company charged to rebuild Can's shared platform | Allow suitable frameworks, libraries, templates and provider services on both sides. Separate app-specific work from reusable platform development and maintenance. Assess platform feasibility and funding explicitly, without counting all shared work anew per adopter or assuming it is free. |
| Proposed Can capabilities treated as working software; or absent implementation treated as proof the language design cannot work | Evaluate design expressibility, coherence, feasibility and prospective value. Current shipping readiness is outside this evaluation. Missing implementation is the declared stage, not a negative finding; execution-dependent claims remain unverified. An actual contract contradiction or required behavior omission remains a design finding. |
| Expert-repaired Can against a single unassisted framework attempt, or unfamiliar Can without documentation against a familiar language | Fix business acceptance outcomes and stopping rules before attempts. Match the brief, model/effort and assistance/repair policy; provide relevant documentation and available tools to each and count their cost. Preserve all bounded attempts and interventions. Report differences in available tooling and pretrained familiarity rather than concealing or artificially eliminating them. |
| Short Can source against all generated JavaScript, or a small edit against regenerating the competing app | Separate authored source, generated artifacts, library code and total interaction tokens. Compare the same initial task or change and required outcomes, including UI, permissions, examples and locales where required. Use each approach's normal efficient edit workflow. |
| SaaS subscription price against Can hosting alone, or suite list price counted as savings for every replaced module | Compare total costs over the same stated horizon and workload, including setup, migration, overlap, support, updates and staff effort on each side. Count only avoidable subscription charges, accounting for bundles, retained seats and commitments. Separate one-time and recurring costs; show sensitivity where estimates could change the conclusion. |
| An untouched incumbent treated as costless, or a clean-slate build charged with migration it does not need | Distinguish a new workflow from replacing an existing system. For replacement, include real switching, training and continuity costs alongside the incumbent's configuration, workarounds, integration and change burden. Past sunk spending is not a future cost; avoidable future work is. |
| Unlimited custom flexibility credited to Can, or an intentionally inflexible incumbent used as its only rival | Include credible available configuration, integrations or low-code options when they serve the selected workflow, alongside retaining the subscription or a partial replacement. Compare task completion, staff effort and useful tailoring; visual novelty and feature count are not substitutes. |
| Non-programmers judged on hand-authoring Can, or company ownership assumed to eliminate technical support and dependency risk | Evaluate staff specifying, reviewing and using their app through the intended interfaces, with maintenance responsibilities and available assistance stated. Compare control, data exit, continuity and dependency risks on both sides; source ownership alone does not establish independence. |
| Familiar architecture accepted without evidence while alternatives must prove themselves, or all uncertainty interpreted against one option | Apply the same outcome criteria and evidence standard to keeping and changing a design. Preserve real differences in maturity and evidence, identify assumptions, and allow inconclusive results. Judge each finding's impact rather than using a broad weighted score to conceal a required capability failure. |

A owns the shared company conditions and economic/experiment comparisons; B and C supply their assigned semantic and interface evidence. E010 records comparison limitations and E101 consolidates them. If a material mismatch remains, qualify or withhold that comparison's verdict rather than generalizing it into support for or rejection of CanLang.

## Working method and deliverable

Complete the checklist in stages. Start by challenging the foundations, then establish audience fit and baseline evidence, then examine representative applications deeply, then check applicable findings across the corpus. A repeated problem should become one finding with several examples, rather than dozens of duplicate app tickets.

Adoption and company-owned tailored business applications are the evaluation's north star. Every technical choice is reviewable, including choices already written into the requirements. Record each choice's origin: explicit user requirement, assistant proposal, inherited convention or measured necessity. Questioning a user requirement means showing its tradeoffs and evidence for discussion; it does not authorize silently overriding it. Brainstorming and audit findings do not authorize a broad conversion.

For every language construct and mandatory rule, ask: What company need does this serve? What evidence supports it? What happens if it is removed? Can an existing construct express the same behavior more economically? What is the simplest credible alternative? Where does its complexity go? What information, safety or flexibility would the alternative lose? Do not preserve a choice merely because drafts depend on it, implementation has begun, or JEV previously preferred it. Require proportional evidence; do not turn these questions into another per-declaration paperwork layer.

Use existing artifacts wherever they answer the question. Make a small, isolated experiment only when inspection leaves a material uncertainty. Do not build a new verification framework, generate another portfolio, or perform speculative feature implementation for this audit. Independent work can run in parallel with clear ownership; workers should receive bounded questions and relevant files.

Each finding records: the company task affected; source evidence; the observed problem; its likely adoption impact; whether it is a language defect, app omission, target inconsistency or documentation defect; and the smallest credible remedy with its tradeoff. Record implementation-dependent uncertainty as an evidence limitation rather than a defect. State uncertainty. Do not present hypothetical concerns as demonstrated defects.

### Improvement feedback for negative findings

Every supported negative result must help improve Can. In the existing finding, record:

- **Cause:** what makes Can less effective for this company task, supported by the available evidence.
- **Proposed improvement:** the smallest concrete change to the language, defaults, contracts, draft, documentation or intended product experience that could address the cause.
- **Expected benefit:** which outcome should improve, such as authoring tokens, reliable edits, staff effort, workflow fit or ownership cost, and why. Label estimates and untested hypotheses.
- **Tradeoff:** added complexity, implementation/maintenance burden, or effects on other workflows; prefer improving or simplifying existing mechanisms where sufficient.
- **Re-evaluation:** the same company task and comparison criteria that would show whether the revised design improves the result, including important regressions to check. Identify what is assessable in drafts and what needs later execution evidence.

Keep one improvement recommendation per consolidated cause, with the existing accountable owner. If no credible remedy is known, explain the limitation and the smallest useful investigation or audience/scope decision; do not invent a feature or promise that every comparison can be won. Preserve the original finding alongside any proposed revision, and attribute an improved result to the design change under unchanged comparison conditions. JEV may assess supplied alternatives and evidence under the existing consultation rules; its probabilities alone do not explain a cause or demonstrate an improvement. Difficult remediation choices belong to E102. The final verdict must include this prioritized improvement path, not only strengths and weaknesses.

Prioritize findings as adoption blockers, significant friction, or polish. Give every proposed remedy a disposition: keep the current rule, clarify it, simplify it, correct an app/target, investigate with a bounded experiment, or defer/exclude it with an explicit audience/scope consequence. Do not require a numerical approval threshold or claim a high aggregate score cancels a serious permission or behavior defect.

Difficult design decisions follow [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md): supply researched evidence to JEV, make three fresh consultations with semantically equivalent alternatives and fully rewritten explanatory prose, retain uncertainty, investigate disagreement, and treat its judgments as advice. This task list itself does not require a consultation on every item.

Before sending each consultation, check the entire request for framing bias: context, instructions, questions, company profiles, criteria and option descriptions. Remove leading questions, loaded labels, assumed conclusions and references to our preferred answer or earlier JEV votes. Give competing options comparable detail and apply the same evidentiary standard to their benefits, costs and uncertainties; include material counterevidence. Preserve genuine evidence differences rather than inventing false balance. Do not select company assumptions to manufacture support for CanLang. Check both semantic equivalence and full-request wording differences across the three versions: paraphrasing a biased premise does not remove its bias, and unanimous results do not prove neutrality.

Use JEV in two explicit roles: a design adviser on difficult alternatives, and a mock company decision maker evaluating the proposed product's prospective value. The adoption evaluation owns the mock-company consultations; other evaluation owners supply relevant findings. JEV is a classifier, not a research or roleplaying LLM: provide the company profile, decision-maker responsibilities, workflow, alternative, constraints, cost estimates, maintenance/support obligations and evidence yourself. Mark hypothetical assumptions and unknowns, and use representative company profiles rather than one favorable invented buyer.

For mock-company decisions, ask whether the described product, if implemented according to its stated contracts under explicit cost/support assumptions, would meet the company's needs and offer worthwhile value. Compare retaining the subscription, a future bounded pilot or the composed tailored solution within that same prospective scenario. Do not ask whether a company should replace live software with today's drafts. Preserve unresolved design gaps and uncertain assumptions; this framing does not grant Can imaginary capabilities or free maintenance. Use `choice` for alternatives, `noul` for yes/no properties and `score` for an ordered rubric. Apply the same three-consultation protocol, including rewriting all explanatory prose and criteria while preserving facts and alternatives; save every request, response and returned probability/uncertainty. Investigate disagreement and sensitivity to material company assumptions. Record these results as simulated product-value judgments, not customer testimony, willingness-to-pay measurements or proof of market demand. Include them in the ownership comparison and design verdict alongside the actual evidence.

- [x] **F007 — Is metadata helping more than it costs?** Challenge mandatory descriptions, labels, translation variants and repeated schema annotations. - [x] **F010 — Does the bounded daisyUI presentation serve real tailoring?** Challenge the shell, component selection, layout limits and theme controls against company use. - [x] **F012 — Does the Cloudflare/workerd commitment help adoption enough?** Evaluate the service reuse, operating model, setup burden, costs and constraints for the intended companies. - [x] **F015 — Could ownership beat the subscription for the selected use case?** Compare the proposed product's expected generation, review, operating, update and support burden with the capability it would replace, under stated implementation and cost assumptions. - [x] **E022 — Measure total task tokens.** Include brief intake, documentation, generation, failed attempts, repairs and later edits. Determine whether syntax compression reduces the complete interaction cost or merely moves explanation into prompts and docs.
- [x] **E023 — Find repeated syntax and structures.** Count repeated attributes, schemas, field lists, operation patterns and subtrees across the corpus. Distinguish repeated keywords from expensive repeated obligations. Rank candidates by demonstrated token savings and affected apps.
- [x] **E024 — Classify repetition by meaning.** Separate common runtime guarantees, conventional presentation, reusable domain logic, genuine per-app policy and independent expected outcomes. Identify what could become implicit without removing business choices, authorization or evidence.
- [x] **E025 — Compare proposed reductions fairly.** For each substantial reduction, show equivalent before/after behavior, measured token changes, semantic tradeoffs and available agent repair evidence. Distinguish defined defaults from unspecified behavior. Fewer lines alone do not establish a benefit, and implicit shared behavior alone does not establish a defect.

## 6. Canonical primitives and defaults

- [x] **E026 — Audit one way per primitive.** Identify every authored route to CRUD, authentication, page definition, business invocation, files and personal settings. Find wrappers, aliases or competing patterns that let agents unnecessarily rebuild the same behavior.
- [x] **E027 — Audit implicit setup.** Confirm that standard runtime, storage, authentication, teams, themes and file policy are declared once through pinned defaults. Identify copied setup and defaults whose meaning changes with file placement or composition.
- [x] **E028 — Audit workflow bookkeeping.** Examine repeated delivery IDs, revisions, outcome records, retries, compensation and continuation handlers. Determine which are common mechanics and which encode irreducible business decisions. Demonstrate a reduction before recommending another primitive.
- [x] **E029 — Audit generated interfaces.** Check that operation signatures, descriptions and schemas supply UI, MCP and integration interfaces without authored copies. Identify separate schemas or registries that could drift, while preserving intentional exported contract boundaries.
- [x] **E030 — Challenge every costly restriction.** For constraints such as file layout, package wrappers, required metadata, bounds and deployment bindings, identify the actual requirement served. Reconsider assistant-created restrictions when their complexity exceeds their demonstrated benefit.

## 7. Grammar and AI predictability

- [x] **E031 — Evaluate statement boundaries.** Check one-space indentation, necessary newlines, inline/block forms and wide declarations against realistic agent edits. Identify ambiguous boundaries and cases where compact formatting causes disproportionate repair work.
- [x] **E032 — Evaluate naming and contextual keywords.** Examine declarations, imports, aliases, enum cases, lexical bindings and fields named like keywords. Confirm one resolution rule, useful errors and no fallback interpretation that silently changes meaning.
- [x] **E033 — Evaluate Given / When / Then meaning.** Confirm the sections organize data/rules, operations and presentation coherently. Check `invariant` against execution `require` and `do`. Determine whether BDD meaning is visible through attached examples rather than being only section branding.
- [x] **E034 — Evaluate query readability and exactness for agents.** Check alias scope, filtering, projection, ordering, grouping, relationship expansion, null behavior and cardinality. Locate cases where agents need excessive parentheses, boilerplate or guessed parser behavior.
- [x] **E035 — Evaluate the language surface.** Inventory keywords, attributes, operators, builtins and exceptions that agents must learn. Identify overlapping forms, arbitrary inconsistencies and features with no demonstrated use. Compare cognitive/context cost with the benefit each feature provides.

## 8. Records, types and evolving company data

- [x] **E036 — Evaluate model ownership.** Check implicit team, parent containment and app ownership against departments, projects, customers and shared company records. Detect unwanted multi-tenant scaffolding, copied identities and scope boundaries that block ordinary workflows.
- [x] **E037 — Evaluate field-type reuse.** Check required/null/default/server-owned fields, enums, constraints, arrays, contracts and reused field types. Confirm normalization and omitted-versus-null updates remain exact without repeatedly declaring the same schema.
- [x] **E038 — Evaluate historical evidence.** Examine archive/delete, immutable snapshots, audit history, corrections and references. Determine when a snapshot is a business requirement and when copied data creates unnecessary layers or permanently inconsistent authorities.
- [x] **E039 — Evaluate existing-data adoption.** Assess CSV intake, duplicate review, reference resolution, files and partial import outcomes for a company leaving a subscription. Identify which data can realistically be imported and where manual review or unsupported conversion must be visible.
- [x] **E040 — Evaluate schema evolution boundaries.** Review additive changes, renames, backfills, destructive changes, pending work and unsupported splits/merges. Distinguish an initial-install limitation from a material obstacle to maintaining an adopted app; do not invent migrations from unknown predecessors.

## 9. Permissions and information disclosure

- [x] **E041 — Evaluate access authoring cost.** Measure the declarations needed for own records, manager scope, departments/locations, private fields and external users. Check whether common policies are compact while actual company choices remain explicit.
- [x] **E042 — Trace one permission through every interface.** Follow reads and mutations through browser forms, aggregates, CSV/files, MCP and delegated calls. Identify broadening caused by presentation helpers, authority reports or cached results.
- [x] **E043 — Evaluate decision authority versus disclosed data.** Review policy/invariant queries, CRUD admission and privileged reports. Confirm the source specifies what data may influence a decision and what may be returned, without recursive policy ambiguity or hidden data leaks.
- [x] **E044 — Evaluate revocation and identity changes.** Follow removed employees, changed company roles, recovered accounts and archived organizations through outstanding work and integrations. Check immediate local denial, bounded propagation, current authorization and preserved history.
- [x] **E045 — Evaluate company administration needs.** Determine which role assignment, invitation, recovery and configuration journeys the target company actually needs. Identify where developer-only provisioning blocks normal operation; also identify unnecessary generic administration surfaces.

## 10. Business workflows and asynchronous work

- [x] **E046 — Trace complete workflow paths.** For representative operations, follow success, rejection, cancellation, correction and recovery as specified. Check that realistic requirements are expressed in the drafts and defined shared contracts rather than present only as unsupported claims in Markdown or an unspecified helper. Do not require implementations of the shared contracts.
- [x] **E047 — Evaluate state and effect ordering.** Examine leading guards, body guards, provisional reads, invariants, hooks and emitted events. Detect equivalent-looking sources with different behavior or target translations that reorder checks and writes.
- [x] **E048 — Evaluate uncertainty and replay.** Follow duplicate deliveries, stale callbacks, transport failures and unknown provider outcomes. Check stable identities and ordered observations; enqueue, payment and business fulfillment must remain distinguishable where they differ.
- [x] **E049 — Evaluate staged replacement and compensation.** Follow a change requiring several authorities. Check whether original commitments remain usable through failure and whether abandoned candidates are eventually released. Identify repeated mechanics that obscure the actual business decision.
- [x] **E050 — Evaluate real progress under growth.** Review `for limit`, periodic scans, fanout and continuation events against accumulating company records. Bounds must not silently truncate or prevent all work once the match count grows. Record throughput, starvation and recovery assumptions.

## 11. Time, money and lifecycle semantics

- [x] **E051 — Evaluate business calendar behavior.** Check local dates, timezones, daylight-saving transitions, inclusive/exclusive boundaries, month-end recurrence and missed work. Determine whether ordinary company deadlines can be expressed without manual calendar infrastructure.
- [x] **E052 — Evaluate quantities and financial meaning.** Check exact arithmetic, units, currency boundaries, confirmed receipts/refunds and balances. Identify invented conversions, misleading totals and common arithmetic that requires excessive authored helpers.
- [x] **E053 — Evaluate retention as a shared guarantee.** Examine expiry, archived/locked evidence, child lifetimes, attachments, replay metadata and local copies. Separate authored business lifetimes from disposal mechanics and verify that null deadlines or delayed cleanup cannot resurrect expired content.
- [x] **E054 — Evaluate replacement of business evidence.** Check revised documents, corrected expenses/time entries and changed commercial terms. Preserve earlier evidence where required and use canonical operations rather than creating another copy-and-review convention per app.
- [x] **E055 — Evaluate reporting truth.** Trace numerator, denominator, as-of boundary, source revision, completeness and freshness. Detect anonymous totals that hide missing producers, unsupported zero values, double-counted reversals or inappropriate precision.

## 12. Composition and integration contracts

- [x] **E056 — Evaluate small-app composition.** Compose two useful small tools without extra wrappers, manifests, duplicate owners or namespace collisions. Confirm identity and behavior are stable standalone, composed and after file moves.
- [x] **E057 — Evaluate larger package boundaries.** Check whether packages group coherent business ownership and help an agent make focused changes. Detect artificial splits, implicit cycles and duplicated models that create operational boundaries without a business reason.
- [x] **E058 — Evaluate local versus deployed imports.** Trace schemas, execution, permissions, pages/tools and resource inclusion for both forms. Determine whether an ordinary company app is accidentally forced into distributed deployment and reconciliation.
- [x] **E059 — Evaluate provider-owned contracts.** Confirm payload types and operations have one authority, compatible versions and explicit mappings. Check that the language implementation need not know each app or third-party vendor's complete API.
- [x] **E060 — Evaluate optional connections.** Check whether a standalone app can omit an unused provider without requiring fake bindings or copied alternate source files. Identify real design limitations and evaluate their adoption impact before proposing optional-binding syntax.

## 13. Cloudflare and shared-library responsibility

- [x] **E061 — Map declared service needs to existing capabilities.** Identify what workerd, JavaScript/web APIs and Cloudflare bindings already provide. Locate proposed reimplementations of encoding, HTTP, storage or other foundations. Research current primary documentation when the evaluation needs provider facts.
- [x] **E062 — Evaluate first-class service consistency.** Compare files, queues, schedules, email, analytics and other declared services. Check compact configuration, typed inputs/results, ownership, inferred defaults and explicit exceptions. Do not adopt a service merely because it exists.
- [x] **E063 — Evaluate compiler versus library boundaries.** Map static checking/lowering, reusable presentation and runtime business guarantees to one owner each. Identify duplicated contracts and abstractions that move complexity without reducing total system cost.
- [x] **E064 — Evaluate infrastructure leakage into apps.** Find SQL, transport wiring, platform boilerplate, provider configuration and technical bookkeeping authored as business logic. Determine what must remain explicit and what should be generated or supplied once.
- [x] **E065 — Evaluate deployment assumptions.** Assess local/composed use, managed bindings, provider accounts, compatibility and environment-specific configuration. Identify surprising mandatory services or authority placements that make a company's simple app expensive to operate.

## 14. Standard shell and frontend completeness

- [x] **E066 — Evaluate the implicit shell.** Confirm collapsible left navigation, top branding, page-derived authorized links and bottom account menu. Check the settings dialog and section navigation without repeated app markup or a second navigation registry.
- [x] **E067 — Evaluate personal configuration.** Review Account, Language, Appearance, Security and actual app preferences. Check persistence, validation, reset, scoped references and business-versus-presentation distinctions. Identify missing logic hidden behind a settings mockup.
- [x] **E068 — Evaluate component sufficiency.** Exercise useful lists, tables, forms, cards, details, tabs, agendas, metrics and empty states. Record demonstrated layouts or interactions that the bounded daisyUI surface cannot express; do not demand arbitrary HTML/CSS as a default escape hatch.
- [x] **E069 — Evaluate binding and schema reuse.** Follow protected record arguments, result scope, field controls, labels, defaults, validation and action availability. Detect repeated input declarations or UI convenience helpers that become a second business API.
- [x] **E070 — Evaluate HTMX and browser state.** Trace form submission, partial responses, drawers, filters, focus and refresh. Separate temporary interface state from business authority. Identify a client-side business-state requirement only when a real supported journey demonstrates it.

## 15. Usability for company staff

- [x] **E071 — Walk actual staff journeys.** Evaluate a new employee completing work, a manager handling an exception and an authorized external user accessing their records. Assess the flow from navigation through action and feedback, rather than counting components.
- [x] **E072 — Evaluate narrow-screen and keyboard use.** Check table/card adaptation, forms, sidebar, dialogs, tabs, focus and accessible feedback. Record which guarantees can be shared and which need authored business presentation.
- [x] **E073 — Evaluate failure presentation.** Distinguish invalid input, stale versions, denied access, pending work, unknown outcomes, missing sources and genuinely empty data. Check useful recovery while preserving inputs and avoiding restricted-data disclosure.
- [x] **E074 — Evaluate common productivity needs.** Assess search, filtering, ordering, pagination, bulk intake/export and useful record detail against the selected company journeys. Identify the minimal missing contract when these need repeated app-specific code.
- [x] **E075 — Evaluate tailoring without UI reinvention.** Change labels, visible fields, groups, priorities and workflow-specific views. Determine whether companies can get an appropriate app within the standard shell and whether restrictions cause repetitive alternative pages.

## 16. MCP and chatbot business operations

- [x] **E076 — Evaluate tool discovery.** Inspect app context, canonical operation names, descriptions and schemas. Determine whether a chatbot can find the right operation without duplicated documentation or app-specific prompting layers.
- [x] **E077 — Evaluate browser/chatbot parity.** Follow the same business action through both interfaces. Check shared validation, permissions, expected versions, outcomes and file/reference handling. A chatbot must not gain a different business API by bypassing the form.
- [x] **E078 — Evaluate operation input economy.** Inspect IDs, versions, action handles, defaults and protected bindings. Determine what the model genuinely needs to supply and which repetitive protocol details belong to generated interfaces.

## 20. Tooling and operating the owned application

- [x] **E096 — Evaluate the future agent feedback loop.** Review the intended checker, lint, format and editor diagnostics as contracts. - [x] **E097 — Evaluate proposed packaging and deployment economy.** Trace the intended source selection, derived dependencies, build, deployment and updates. - [x] **E099 — Evaluate continuity of company ownership.** Review backup/restore, export, staff turnover, configuration recovery and source/provider version changes at the contract level. ## 21. Synthesis and a finite improvement plan

- [x] **E101 — Consolidate root causes.** Merge repeated symptoms into one evidenced finding. Separate language-design problems from workspace-specific policy, unfinished app logic, JavaScript drift and implementation absence. Record remaining uncertainty explicitly.
- [x] **E102 — Compare simplification alternatives.** For high-impact findings, compare retaining/clarifying the current design, changing a default, improving an existing primitive, adding a narrow primitive and excluding a use case. Include costs and disadvantages for every alternative; consult JEV where the decision is difficult.
- [x] **E103 — Prioritize by company adoption.** Rank remedies by their effect on successful generation, cost, change effort, staff usefulness and trust. Consider implementation/maintenance cost and frequency of use. A rare elaborate app must not dictate the simplest app's authored complexity.
- [x] **E104 — Define the next bounded correction pass.** Turn negative findings into a prioritized improvement path: exact causes and proposed changes, affected contracts/sources, expected company benefit, tradeoffs and re-evaluation under the original comparison conditions. Give independent work clear ownership and resolve dependencies promptly. Further discoveries enter the consolidated list without silently expanding every worker's scope.
- [x] **E105 — State the product-design verdict.** Explain which company use cases the proposed design supports convincingly, which evidenced design problems hinder prospective adoption, and which refinements or later validation would resolve them. State the conditions behind value and feasibility conclusions. Do not recommend dropping Can, label it an adoption failure or declare the draft deficient because implementation has not begun. Unverified execution claims remain unverified; they are not failed tests. Produce a finite design assessment and correction plan, not a shipping-readiness verdict.

## Audit completion

The evaluation is complete when every task has an evidence-backed result or a justified not-applicable/implementation-dependent status; repeated findings are consolidated; supported negative findings include actionable improvement feedback or an explained limit on available remedies; major disagreements and difficult decisions retain their evidence; and the user has one prioritized, finite improvement plan to discuss.

This does not require every proposed remedy to be implemented during the audit. It requires a supported assessment of which conditions could make company-owned tailored SaaS economical to generate, use and change, where the design falls short, and which conclusions remain unverified. A draft-only evaluation cannot establish production economics or adoption where execution and company evidence are unavailable.
