# Consultation framing

Research advisory only; no grammar or provider has been selected by these requests.

All three requests retain the same facts: draft-only freedom; AI-authored company apps; adoption and token economy; Given/When/Then roles; current typed imports and durable final-completion semantics; missing stream/progress/cancel contracts; existing workerd APIs and separate hosted services; reusable JavaScript libraries; the same six AI/business use cases; provider feature differences; company-owned policy; and absence of implementation measurements.

Every explanatory context, question instruction and option description was independently rewritten. Technical identifiers and the alternative keys stay stable. Before sending, the generic alternative was corrected in all three requests: it may extend the common execution lifecycle too. Restricting that alternative to existing final-only callbacks would have unfairly favored shared domain contracts. All alternatives may share transport and lifecycle implementation. The disputed boundary is provider-shaped interfaces versus library-owned domain contracts versus dedicated language forms.

The choice type compares these three alternatives; it is not an approval threshold or an accuracy claim. Retain complete answers and probabilities. Agreement will not settle the design; the next evidence is the authored app/desired-JavaScript pairs and their provider mismatch cases.

This is not unanimous advice. Inspection of request 2 found no different requirement or alternative: its generic option emphasizes retaining a common execution system, while its shared option describes careful mismatch/lifecycle design. Requests 1 and 3 communicate the same costs and benefits in different prose. That change in emphasis may affect the result; the classifier supplies no rationale that would establish why it changed. Do not infer a causal explanation, average away the disagreement, or choose by two-to-one vote.

The decision to test remains narrower than adoption: share external-call lifecycle machinery in either approach; compare provider-shaped and standard domain interfaces in one chat/image pair and one email-classification pair. Count caller-authored mappings, repeated schema/field declarations, and special branches needed for unsupported features. In particular, compare a fixed typed question batch with a declaration that derives its output enum/probability schema from its authored criteria. If a standard interface merely moves large option maps into every app, it has not demonstrated the intended advantage.

All runs assigned low probability to dedicated core syntax, but that is conditional advice about these alternatives and facts, not a rule against adding a concise declaration where the side-by-side drafts justify one. The requests do not settle the detailed syntax of question batches, provider configuration, streaming or durable continuation.
