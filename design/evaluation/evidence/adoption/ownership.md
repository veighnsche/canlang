## First useful workflow setup

REQUIREMENTS.md:75–89 and DESIGN.md:15–19,39–45,95–99 pin shared runtime/authentication/team/D1/UI/MCP and permit a one-file implicit app. Small J1 need not author database/auth/UI manifests, email, billing or telemetry accounts. Its actual business owner/department policies remain app source. DESIGN.md:81–91 derives dependency inclusion; imported whole executable owners may add resource requirements even with unmounted pages, while message-only imports do not. Minimal source should not import the entire CanDo workspace federation merely to create an equipment register; TeamTasks is the correct small reference. An installation still needs supported provider account, actual company/team/role assignments, supported release, source/configuration retention and authorized steward. This is intended product setup responsibility, not staff hand-programming.

CanExpense is an operator-specific draft, not a universal expense-product template: employee/location imports and can_work guards are chosen company policy (CanExpense.can:7–24), so a department variant can use the same team/model/role/policy primitives without adopting rooms/member billing. Team default grants no permission (DESIGN.md:95–99). Location vocabulary in this app is not automatically a language-default bias. Important scope decisions—current employee, manager departments, receipt disclosure, evidence policy—cannot be safely invented by the agent. Company P reviewer must approve them in business language.

## Reviewable company result

Canonical descriptions/#, independently expected examples, generated schema labels, form inputs, action/status and shared interface feedback make business outcomes potentially reviewable (REQUIREMENTS.md:39–67; DESIGN.md:349–411,553–657). They are evidence of the intended experience, not observed staff comprehension. The smallest review artifact is one generated business-readable change/permission summary plus selected success/denial/recovery examples and unresolved assumptions, before deployment approval. It should derive from the owning source and examples rather than duplicate target JS documentation. Tradeoff: compiler/product work and risk of misleading summaries; compare to authorized staff reviewing the same outcomes in incumbent configuration, including expected EUR500/EUR501 paths, department leak, stale/replay cases. No reader must inspect handwritten MJS to approve business meaning.

## Continuity contracts and real limits

Schema/source version pins and exact predecessors are explicit (DESIGN.md:743–770). Nullable field addition is conditionally automatic; renames/backfills/removals are explicit and cannot erase data silently. Source/provider contract changes and old queued work need release inventory/drain/reconcile (DESIGN.md:818–824). Team/parent/storage transfer, split/merge and arbitrary job conversion are deliberately outside v1 (DESIGN.md:768,822); record them as scope limits for restructuring an adopted company, not generic failures of ordinary additive edits. Under P optional cost centre/rename, existing supported mechanisms suffice prospectively. Under a real later company merger/split requiring scope transfer, retain supported stewardship/manual external transformation until a bounded future contract is justified; source ownership alone is not a promise of that capability.

Data exit has scoped generated CSV export with checkpoint/completeness, currency/reference versions, formula protection and large jobs (DESIGN.md:603) plus authorized immutable files. This makes routine records review/portability plausible. CSV alone cannot recreate source, roles, provider secrets, uploaded bytes, audit/replay/queued work and exact release. The inspected Can design defines evolution/activation but does not yet give the company a single explicit whole-application backup/restore/responsibility contract. This is an underdescribed intended ownership experience, not missing implementation counted as a defect. Smallest improvement: clarify generated installation/operations plan with named steward, source/configuration/version/file/data inventory, backup scope, recovery objectives and exact restore/resume/reconcile steps, reusing provider support and existing fences/retention. No new DSL keyword or blanket atomic restore claim. Benefit: turnover/incident continuity and accurate support pricing; tradeoff: product/operator documentation and provider-specific integration/testing. Re-evaluate P restore of receipts/roles/schema and W interrupted booking/payment recovery under the SAME authority, historical-evidence and no-double-charge outcomes; actual recovery time/success requires later execution.

## Essential organization requirements for these companies

P/W require identity/member/role administration and revocation, private employee/customer records/files, evidence attribution, historical corrections, current authorization across UI/MCP/export, source/provider secret management, deploy/version inventory, backup/exit and contractual technical support. Browser accessibility/localization and staff account turnover matter. Residency, retention duration, acceptable outage/recovery time and domain-deliverability expectations require company-specific decisions; do not assert compliance/certifications, choose arbitrary residency or silently charge enterprise SSO. SSO/SAML, SCIM, detailed legal certifications, payroll/statutory reporting and enterprise SLA are not stated essential for P/W. If a real buyer requires them, scope/pricing needs explicit review rather than pretending defaults satisfy them. Customer/company invitation/removal serves W's real portal; reselling Can to many unrelated SaaS tenants and building vendor subscription administration is not required (WORKSPACE_OPERATOR.md and PORTFOLIO authoritative ownership). Specialized offline navigation/route optimization remains required for Company S's full suite and outside the CRUD-first bet (REQUIREMENTS.md:13–21); partial module value cannot be full subscription savings.
