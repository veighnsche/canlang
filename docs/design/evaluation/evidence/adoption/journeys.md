# Shared company conditions and acceptance journeys

Version: 2026-10-04. Author: owner A. These are deliberately hypothetical process briefs, not interviews, demand evidence or measured willingness to pay. All lanes use frozen `baseline-20261004T041647Z/snapshot/` as source. Company outcomes are pinned before judging drafts; unsupported required steps remain in the comparison. Missing implementation is outside the design-defect count. A composed solution is one company alternative; modules are not separate subscription savings or necessarily separate deployments.

## Company P — ordinary department intake and expense approvals

A 25-person professional-services company has one office and occasional remote staff. Employees use browser forms or an authorized chatbot. An operations lead approves policies; finance approves reimbursements; a contracted technical steward assists installation, migration, quarterly updates and incidents. Staff can describe/review work but do not author Can. AI access is assumed to include a supported agent and secure approved business-data handling; actual organizational policy is unknown. Current tools are a configurable table/workflow subscription and a separate expense product. Payroll, statutory accounting, bank payment, office email and identity remain external. We compare 24 future months, 25 active users, 500 ordinary records and 80 expenses/month; up to 20 attachments/day. Existing data: 2,000 records and 2,000 receipts. Confidential receipts require employee-own, scoped manager and finance access. No asserted certification or regulated health data. Hypothetical subscription totals and support rates will be scenario variables, not vendor claims.

### J1 — small useful CRUD tool (CanDo / TeamTasks reference)

Replace the shared department equipment-request register: employee creates title and note, updates/archive own unresolved request; manager sees department requests; another employee cannot read/edit private requests. Operations exports only authorized rows and imports CSV with invalid/duplicate row review. A table, filter/search, create/edit/archive action and readable outcome must support both browser and chatbot through the same operation. Variant: requests over EUR 250 need an expense/purchase link; do not invent automatic purchasing or include it in the first-app experiment. Acceptance: 8 cases (create; own update; other employee denial; manager scope; invalid title; archive behavior; scoped export; partial import review). Baseline CanDo and examples/TeamTasks are the generic task references, not an assumption that their authored policy exactly matches this company variation. CanTable actually models café reservations and physical occupancy; its initially inferred generic meaning was incorrect and corrected after source inspection. J1 outcomes stay unchanged; no required case was dropped. CanTable remains an additional domain-specific occupancy case, not the smallest CRUD app.

### J2 — private expense lifecycle (CanExpense)

Employee drafts EUR expense with a valid receipt, submits immutable evidence, corrects a rejected claim through a new revision, and sees progress. A manager reviews claims in their scope without self-approval; finance finalizes reimbursement evidence; payroll/bank payment stays external. Duplicate requests, a stale review version, employee revocation and failed delivery retain a recoverable truthful outcome. Ordinary-rule change: amounts above EUR 500 require finance approval after manager approval; threshold is strictly >500 (exactly EUR 500 does not trigger) and EUR-only, no currency conversion. Data change: optional cost centre added; a later rename must preserve old records. Permission change: temporary reviewer may see department A only until revocation. Acceptance: submission, scoped review, self-review denial, stale denial, correction history, one reimbursement evidence, revocation and browser/MCP parity. Do not infer implemented execution from source or desired MJS.

## Company W — composed workspace operator replacement

An independently run two-location workspace operator has 12 staff, 120 customer organizations, 600 member accounts, 30 rooms and 100 pooled desks. Its operations director approves replacement and customer continuity; an external technical steward handles deployment and provider updates. Staff use browser/MCP; customer company administrators and invited members use a limited portal. Current workspace subscription covers its REQUIRED customer/company records, commercial proposal-to-room reservation, issued invoice collection, membership access, reception and physical-mail workflow. Unused suite capabilities are irrelevant. This evaluation selects a bounded short-rental/customer flow plus physical mail; a full subscription exit requires all additional REQUIRED membership/reception paths to pass, so selected-path coverage alone cannot justify full savings. Retain accounting/payroll, payment processor, outbound email, physical access vendor and support until avoidable contracts/seats are identified. Compare 24 months: 3,000 bookings/month, 500 issued invoices/month, 300 physical mail items/month, 30,000 outbound notifications/month; existing 20,000 bookings and 5,000 invoices must preserve evidence and customer linkage. Shared identity, owning records, scopes, navigation, consolidated reporting, recovery and coordinated changes are required across modules. No duplicate customer CRUD or independent room calendar. One deployment is a permitted architecture, not a guaranteed result.

### J3 — composed commercial booking (CanCustomer + CanPropose + CanRent + CanInvoice)

Authorized operator creates a scoped customer organization/contact, proposes immutable priced EUR room terms, customer accepts, the room authority reserves one available resource, and the invoice authority confirms exact collection before entitlement/fulfilment. Conflicting reservation, stale accepted offer, unknown payment outcome, callback replay, failed coordination and later cancellation must not double charge/book or erase historical terms. Customer access never grants staff powers. Reporting distinguishes accepted offer, reserved room, confirmed collection and physical arrival; missing producer shows unavailable/partial. Acceptance: success; contention; stale version; replay; unknown payment status; compensation preserving earlier commitment; customer scope; source-truth report; navigation across owning apps. Coordinate provider mappings without duplicating shared records. Selected sketches do not establish every wider WORKSPACE_OPERATOR acceptance journey.

### J4 — asynchronous physical mail (CanMail)

Staff registers one physical item for an authorized customer recipient; notification is queued once with fixed recipient/wording; recipient or current delegate collects OR staff forwards once with evidence. Duplicate event, revoked delegate, stale handle, unknown external outcome and retry remain distinguishable. A mail delegate has no room/membership entitlement. Ordinary integration change replaces outbound notification provider while preserving the owner operation, event identity, permissions and immutable content; actual carrier delivery stays external. Acceptance: intake, recipient/delegate scope, collect, forward, mutual exclusion, duplicate delivery, revocation, retry/status truth, file disclosure and browser/MCP parity.

## Company S — scope counterexample and ordinary second department

A 40-person maintenance contractor needs asset inspections, job assignments, parts receipt and manager reports (ordinary record/workflow fit; CanMaintain/CanField/CanStock/CanPurchase are references). It ALSO needs optimal fleet route planning and offline maps for a region with unreliable coverage. Compare 24 months, 200 jobs/week, 5,000 assets and 15 field workers. Browser/MCP record workflows alone do not replace the whole required field suite if offline maps and optimization are essential. Keep the route/offline subscription or exclude that replacement claim; do not expand the language because a draft exists. A job-specific record app may still be valuable as a partial replacement. Technical support and account management remain real costs. This case tests audience breadth without treating specialized unsupported scope as failure of all CRUD companies.

## Common success criteria and evidence boundaries

A valid replacement completes all required paths, authorization/disclosure, data continuity, exception/recovery, useful interfaces and scoped reporting at the stated workload. Fewer source tokens are useful only with retained outcomes. Reviewers must see business-readable permissions, state transitions, exceptions and unresolved assumptions; source ownership alone does not provide autonomy, backup or support. Compare incumbent configuration/integration or low-code option, a bounded future Can pilot and composed Can replacement with same outcomes, horizon and available support. Include switching/training/overlap, retained subscriptions, changes and support on both sides. Separately report authored source, generated/desired artifacts, shared platform work, request context and total interaction tokens. No company acceptance, runtime correctness, compiler translation or production economics is measured by this audit.
