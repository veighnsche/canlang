# History and retention

Mutation history and operation receipts serve different purposes. History records committed business changes; receipts prevent a retried request from executing twice and retain its outcome for replay. Neither should be mistaken for a complete backup or an indefinite archive. Audit history defaults to 90 days, and longer business evidence should be modeled as records with an explicit lifetime.

CRUD delete archives by default, including contained records. Archived data is not editable and cannot be newly referenced, but existing references may remain for authorized history. Explicit `delete=remove` permanently removes an unreferenced record/subtree; `delete=none` disables deletion. See [record lifecycle](../05-Workflows/01-crud-and-record-lifecycle.md).

`retain Model until=...` declares a pure `datetime?` deadline in `row` scope. It cannot depend on sliding `now`, caller identity, operation state, or randomness. Null gives no independent deadline; a containing parent's finite deadline still applies. A child's finite deadline cannot exceed its parent's. Expiry is irreversible: extending a deadline cannot resurrect already expired content. At expiry, content becomes unavailable immediately even while bounded physical cleanup waits on references or storage. Every stored incoming reference outside the removal set blocks physical removal, including archived or expired referrers. Uniqueness remains reserved until disposal; a conflicting key whose cleanup is blocked returns retryable `busy`. Local audit, receipt, and outbox copies obey the same declared content lifetime. A replay may return `result=null` while retaining safe outcome/identity metadata; it never reruns the mutation to recreate expired content. Locked evidence may expire under its declared lifetime. External copies or already accepted provider content cannot be recalled by local retention. Operators need visibility into blocked cleanup; see [operation](../10-Deployment/05-operating-an-app.md).
