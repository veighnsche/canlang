# CanLang handbook: missing features, buried information and example gaps

Research audit of the canonical public handbook and live website, 20 October 2026. All 86 published Markdown pages were reviewed against the language specifications, accepted feature contracts, compiler/parser, UI and value catalogs, runtime/tooling owners and application examples. This is a maintainer report, outside the public export.

The central failure is substantive: the handbook usually names a feature and describes its restrictions without showing readers how to use it. Expanding the filetree and adding diagrams did not produce a usable language handbook.

## What is visible on the website

The live [CanLang landing page](https://sodaos.dev/canlang) and [handbook](https://sodaos.dev/docs/canlang) were inspected directly in a browser.

- The landing page already shows `$"..."`, `$Name`, named/inline traits, a custom keyword, named/inline styles and prompted views. It also gives a clearer save/check/preview/sign-in first-app journey than the canonical handbook. Useful teaching exists, but outside its primary documentation home.
- The live handbook still displays the older **13-page** structure and identifies source snapshot `02470b7483c1993fc68357860f727111edeb7b2a`. It does not expose the repository's 86-page structure.
- The landing page's “How $ generation works” link leads to the old combined [Services and build-time generation](https://sodaos.dev/docs/canlang/services-and-generation) article. That article has two small trait/style declaration examples; the newer canonical generation chapter has none.
- The live sidebar groups generation under “Services and build-time generation.” There is no dedicated visible `$` learning destination.
- The source [route migration plan](public/FILETREE.md) maps the retired `services-and-generation` route to `contracts-and-service-bindings`. That loses the generation intent of the landing-page link. A future snapshot refresh must update that caller to the generation destination, not blindly apply the generic redirect.

These are two separate defects: stale publication and insufficient canonical content. Publishing the new tree alone would not fix the teaching failure. No website files or generated HTML were changed during this audit.

## How to interpret the detailed findings

- **Missing** means accepted syntax or behavior is not taught in the public handbook.
- **Mentioned only** means a name, abstract signature or rule exists, but readers cannot author the feature from it.
- **Buried** means the answer lives in reference-only prose, specifications, contributor instructions or test fixtures without a useful path from the task.
- **Incomplete journey** means individual pieces exist but the reader cannot connect prerequisites, source, commands, observable result and recovery.
- **Product-surface question** means the intended behavior is accepted but the supported public operation/setup contract is not sufficiently settled to write honest instructions. It is not permission to invent commands or expose private implementation files.

The intended finished product remains the coverage boundary. An unfinished implementation is not grounds to omit an accepted feature. Conversely, a draft business application's company-specific behavior is not automatically a language guarantee.

The existing [PromptStudio application](../tests/fixtures/prompt-pipeline/PromptStudio.can) contains named and inline model/field traits, a genuine custom keyword, named and inline styles, a row-bound renderer and authored acceptance examples. The source is evidence and material to adapt into small tutorials; its large production prompts should not be pasted wholesale into a beginner page.

The `$` teaching failure is therefore not lack of a product feature or lack of available source. The handbook omitted the usable source forms from the places readers need them.

## Information architecture and misleading promises

1. **A large tree substitutes for explanatory depth.** Many “owner” pages have an introduction, one heading and one dense paragraph. A separate filename does not mean the subject is covered.
2. **Reference sends readers back to prose that lacks syntax.** The syntax reference lists construct names and directs readers to owner pages or the normative GitHub grammar. Many owner pages also omit actual declarations. The reader must leave the handbook to reconstruct ordinary code.
3. **Some cross-links promise content that is absent.** Descriptions/app-reference promises reusable-message and translation forms in the syntax reference; that destination has neither. Types/values points to operators, but the referenced builtin inventory is not an operator table.
4. **Important introductory choices live in late reference chapters.** Runtime context, complete value signatures, service schemas and generation settings should remain precise reference, but their first use needs a local example and a direct link.
5. **Recipes do not deliver their named task.** Their constraints are useful, but an approval/booking/sync recipe must include actual models, rules, interface, examples and outcomes. Calling an outline a recipe overstates its utility.
6. **Core generation and UI concepts are combined too tightly.** Traits versus custom keywords and styles versus custom renderers need separate worked explanations even if their filenames stay stable. Settings UI screenshots do not teach the `preferences` declaration.
7. **The handbook lacks an example discovery surface.** Complete apps, reusable fragments and design outlines are not distinguished in an accessible gallery or task index. Useful examples exist under `examples/` and test fixtures, but readers are not guided to them.
8. **Visual work has outpaced source teaching.** Diagrams explain relationships and screenshot briefs describe future captures; neither answers what code to write. Keep useful visuals, but place them after a concrete authoring explanation.
9. **The requested summary contract is still incomplete.** 67 opening paragraphs are outside the length range. Several introductions carry technical detail that should be in meaningful body sections. This contributes to poor page previews, but shortening them must not replace adding missing substance.

## Repair order justified by the findings

1. Complete a coherent first-app path and a small `$` extension tutorial, with real source, commands, visible outcomes and expected refusal cases.
2. Teach language expressions/data/rules and screen composition through a small continuing app; add a real syntax/operator reference and explicit links at first use.
3. Turn each recipe into an authored, checked example. Reuse the existing app corpus where appropriate, after removing stale assumptions and verifying behavior.
4. Add complete service/AI/delivery and testing/deployment journeys, resolving the public-surface questions below instead of inventing commands.
5. Refresh the website snapshot and routes from the improved canonical source. Keep specific links such as “How $ generation works” pointed at their actual subject.

This is a recommended order, not an instruction to begin a rewrite during this audit. Three independently phrased JEV consultations favored practical examples/journeys over navigation-first or reference-first repair. That advice supports the priority; source evidence establishes the gaps.

## Questions that require a public product contract

Most findings need no owner decision: accepted syntax and usable examples already exist. The following need an explicit supported public workflow before exact tutorial commands can be written:

- How application authors provision/select deployment environments and resources without hand-authoring private preparation sidecars.
- How users obtain the installed predecessor identity and perform a complete migration/activation/recovery through public controls.
- Which production operator interface owns pending-work inspection, cancellation/reconciliation, health and retained-file maintenance.
- The intended local preview persistence and schema-change behavior across edits/restarts, stated concretely enough for a first-change tutorial.

These do not justify dropping final-product behavior from the handbook. They identify where product owners must supply a stable author-facing contract.
