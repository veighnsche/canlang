# CanKnowledge — reviewed procedures and private sourced answers

The complete [Can source](CanKnowledge.can) and [desired JavaScript](CanKnowledge.mjs) are a design-stage application, not a deployed retrieval service. [The accepted corpus contract](../design/complex-apps/knowledge.md#corpus-contract) defines the new narrow primitive, generated interface, grounded-value protection, fixtures and provider boundary. Shared DESIGN/GRAMMAR own language semantics; no compiler, standard-library, index adapter or renderer implementation is claimed.

## Company outcome

An accountable owner maintains a procedure, another person approves its exact immutable revision, and authorized employees ask questions about the published guidance in a Topic. Answers show supporting source revisions and exact citations. The application distinguishes current guidance, an answer from an earlier publication scope, incomplete indexing, unsupported questions and uncertain execution. An employee may explicitly share an unresolved question with the Topic's expert; resolution refers to a current publication the employee can read.

The app owns publication/audience decisions, private question history, hard request limits and expert escalation. The corpus primitive owns extraction, indexing, candidate selection, current source authorization, proof-bound generated content and shared observable-run behavior. Application authors do not recreate chunks, embeddings, copied ACLs, per-token event logs, index job queues or answer caches.

## Topic readership and responsibilities

The standard team/auth system supplies membership and three package roles. `knowledge_manager` configures Topics, assigns their current expert, selects model/policy profiles and limits, manages explicit Audience grants and replaces a departing procedure author. `knowledge_author` can start a Document only in a Topic they can read, and create revisions only for their own active Document. `knowledge_reviewer` can review procedure evidence, publish or withdraw guidance, and pause/restore a Document. An approver must differ from the revision's immutable author even when someone holds multiple roles.

Topic names, responsible experts and configured limits are visible to members. Procedure readership requires a current explicit Audience grant or being the current Topic expert. Authors can read their owned drafting/history; reviewers can inspect procedures for publication. Managers receive only the procedure/publication metadata needed for ownership maintenance unless independently granted content access. A manager role does not silently grant all draft files or private questions.

Ordinary employees see only active Documents and their current published Revision. Earlier revision content remains available only to its authorized editors, not every past reader. Source read/field/file policies control retrieval too. Topic or Document deactivation removes source eligibility immediately, independently of index deletion. A person cannot gain source access through a vector hit, source title, citation, opaque ID, approved model result, template prompt or another person's question.

Questions are private to their asking account. Escalation is explicit disclosure: the employee checks the share option, supplies an explanation and shares the exact question with the Topic's accountable expert and successors. It does not disclose the generated answer, provider receipt or complete private request/budget payload. Changing the expert changes subsequent escalation access; prior expertise is not a lasting grant. Revoking readership preserves the employee's own question/transport history but withholds dependent grounded answers. Standard current membership checks still apply.

## Immutable publication and bounded source content

A Document stays in its original Topic and has one responsible author. Each revision has a unique increasing number (at most 100 per Document), immutable title/body/finalized files and author/time. Text is bounded to 20,000 Unicode scalars; the title to 200. A revision needs nonblank text or at least one attachment. At most four PDF/plain-text files of 5 MiB each are accepted, subject to ordinary finalized-file and attachment authority. Sources can therefore contain at most 20 MiB of attachments per revision. Invalid, foreign or incomplete uploads reject rather than creating a fake file.

Publication creates a reasoned immutable decision attributed to another reviewer. There is at most one active Publication per Document, and one Publication per revision. Publishing a replacement deactivates the previous decision and records its withdrawal actor/time/reason atomically; the previous Revision is unchanged. Withdrawn decisions cannot be reactivated. A desired correction is a new revision and review. Pausing/restoring a Document changes eligibility without deleting its history. Active-publication selection derives `Document.current`; no circular fixture or independently mutable current-revision copy is needed.

Publication establishes company approval, not index readiness. The shared corpus maintains an exact revision/content identity and reports excluded, pending, ready, failed or unavailable. Files are extracted through the binding's supported, bounded extractor; failures and unsupported content are visible. The app never pretends a queued upsert is indexed or a delete acknowledgment removes read authority. An authorized editor can request shared repair of the exact current revision, with a one-per-revision/minute limit; this cannot publish a draft or change the source.

Domain records have no ordinary delete path or automatic retention expiry in this draft. Historic material retains its restrictive grants. A later organizational disposal policy must explicitly handle source files, outstanding provider obligations and protected answer provenance; it must not accidentally turn stale evidence into a current answer.

## The corpus primitive

`Handbook` declares the local Revision model, Topic scope, title, body/files and `live(row)` eligibility once. Its generated typed `answer`, `cancel`, `reconcile`, `refresh`, `status` and `available` operations/reads use the existing call/effect structure. Source policies remain the authority; the declaration does not install alternate ACLs or grant anonymous search. The JS target contains one matching corpus descriptor and references generated types instead of copying an output schema.

The binding admits a compatible model/policy profile, bounded extraction and proof-preserving retrieval. It may use Vectorize or a compatible managed service, but cannot enable a vendor public answer endpoint or global content cache that bypasses current checks. Its source selection is limited to the declared local model; arbitrary website crawling, user URLs, tools and cross-provider connectors are outside this app.

Before retrieval and dispatch, derive the genuine caller/team, current eligible authorized source set and exact content revisions. Metadata is a prefilter, not a read grant. Fetch/recheck selected source fields/files under the original principal before giving excerpts to a model. Any filter-size constraint needs a bounded equivalent partition strategy or a visible failure, never a broader unfiltered query. Inputs and source documents are untrusted evidence; they cannot change the grounding policy, call tools, substitute credentials or turn embedded instructions into operation authority.

This pinned profile supports at most 1,000 eligible source revisions per Topic, at most 12 context sources and 64,000 extracted Unicode scalars per question, as well as the stricter model token and source-file limits. Oversized work fails rather than silently reporting a partial scan as complete. `coverage=complete` means the currently authorized eligible source set was confirmed indexed at that retrieval checkpoint; it does not mean every relevant sentence was retrieved or every claim is true. Failed/pending/unsupported sources make coverage incomplete. An unavailable service does not become “no documents.” With no usable/supporting evidence, complete successfully with no answer and a safe unsupported explanation.

## Protected answers, exact citations and later change

An answer is an opaque generated `Handbook.Answer`, associated with its original delivery. Application code cannot extract `.text`, copy a citation array into an ordinary record, construct the value, concatenate it or pass its body to another model. The shared content component and canonical read/MCP serialization expose its bounded statements/citations under current proof checks. `read_answer` is the explicit private read operation. Source policies and opaque provenance remain effective even if a raw receipt handle or earlier page URL is known.

The controller validates source identity, extraction revision, exact quoted spans/locators, citation membership and per-statement citations before minting the grounded value. It retains every context source shown to the model, including inputs the model did not cite. Titles come from authorized source records; model URLs/HTML are not accepted navigation. Up to 20 bounded statements and 12 exact context sources are allowed. Source-backed model prose remains an unreviewed suggestion, not a proof of entailment or an approved replacement procedure. Employees can inspect the original authorized sources.

| Change after generation | Subsequent answer display |
| --- | --- |
| A used source loses read/file permission, is withdrawn, deactivated or superseded | Withhold the entire answer and every citation, including text influenced by uncited context. Safe run/usage history remains. |
| An unrelated/new authorized publication changes the visible scope; all used sources remain valid | Retain the exact-source answer with a prominent historical/as-of label and original retrieval checkpoint. Ask again explicitly to obtain a current answer. |
| No relevant source or scope change | Display the answer with its original evidence and coverage. |
| Proof/storage recheck fails technically | Fail closed and show unavailable; do not mislabel it as unsupported or a successful empty answer. |
| Browser reconnect, old response URL, cache or history read | Apply the same current proof, viewer and source checks; never replay previously rendered content to bypass them. |

The full used-context fence is mandatory. Broader scope change is metadata for historical presentation, not automatic erasure of a still-authorized answer. The opaque value's renderer makes that distinction without another language keyword or a copied app field. A partial answer retains its incomplete-coverage label even when its used sources remain valid. The original caller must still be the viewer in this initial profile; sharing an answer with another employee is not supported. Already delivered bytes or human memories cannot be retroactively erased, but every subsequent app disclosure is checked.

## Requests, limits and recovery

A Topic chooses a pinned model/policy profile and hard per-call input/output/time caps. Defaults are 8,192 input tokens, 2,048 output tokens and 120 seconds; maxima are 32,768/4,096 tokens and 10 minutes. The request snapshots these settings and the trimmed nonblank question (at most 4,000 scalars), with the operation identity as stable source. Later Topic configuration changes never mutate an admitted request. The binding also enforces its provisioned finite index/model resource limits; deployment credentials or unsupported models cannot be supplied in a question.

Each member has a UTC-day request count per Topic (default 20, configurable 1–100) and at most two unfinished questions across the app. Count the durable request in the same transaction before outbox submission. Failed, uncertain and skipped requests retain their consumed daily slot; this simple allocation never invents a billing refund. At maximum settings the daily count multiplies the explicit per-call token bound; provider currency invoices remain outside the app. Shared commit fencing prevents concurrent admissions from exceeding these limits.

Associated progress preserves nullable/no-evidence, queued, running, succeeded, failed, unknown and cancelled states. A verified terminal progress notification releases the unfinished-question count idempotently, without copying text or maintaining another sequence/state machine. Measured usage is displayed when known; missing usage does not mean zero. Stopping requests cancellation of precisely the original identity and records the request, not confirmed cancellation. Unknown/failed calls and races preserve the truthful state. Reconnect/polling only reads existing progress and does not generate again.

Reconciliation observes the original question without a new model request, at most three explicit checks and no overlapping pending check. It remains available while the Topic is inactive or readership has changed, under ownership/membership; dependent answer content remains subject to current proof. A proven undispatched/skipped original receipt can explicitly release the unfinished count. Unknown execution cannot be manually relabeled skipped/cancelled to create room. Exhausted recovery remains visible operator work; publication/manual browsing and expert escalation do not depend on model availability.

## Expert resolution

The current Topic expert sees only explicitly escalated questions and explanations. Resolution accepts either a current published Revision in the same Topic that the requester can read, or no revision plus an attributable closure explanation. A draft, withdrawn/superseded revision, another Topic or loss of requester readership rejects the source-backed resolution. A closed escalation is immutable; a later unresolved concern is a new explicitly shared escalation, with at most one open issue per question.

The requester can read safe resolution metadata. A source-backed resolution note, source body and files are shown only while that referenced Revision is still current and permitted. The page selects the referenced revision through an ordinary authorized query before reading its content, so a withheld revision does not break the private question/status view. If it later becomes unavailable, the note is withheld too: expert response text cannot serve as a second uncontrolled copy of old guidance. The record retains the resolution reference and attribution. An expert's intentional human closure explanation is separately authored business communication, never an automatic copy of generated content.

## Screens and behavior witnesses

Four bilingual shared-shell pages provide authorized procedure browsing and private questions, author/reviewer evidence, expert questions, and administrative readership/model budgets. Every page carries breadcrumbs and every collection an explicit empty state and pagination; question progress and escalation states render as badges, delivery as status, and disclosure sections use collapses. The main page shows index readiness, current source text/files, request/coverage/usage, safe errors, the protected answer/citations and historical warning, cancellation/reconciliation and explicit sharing. Editors see their permitted immutable revision/publication history, and open author/availability changes through catalog modals with typed controls. Managers can replace an author through metadata-only controls. Five-second polling does not invoke providers. Canonical operations supply the same UI and MCP inputs/authority; no client business-state store or custom renderer is introduced.

Inline table examples cover author/independent-review authority, finalized-file attachment ownership, publication withdrawal, exhausted/disabled/revoked-reader question admission, stop/recovery ownership and bounds, idempotent terminal progress, explicit escalation consent and current-publication expert resolution. Four real shared-state sequences read a valid answer, then respectively revoke readership, withdraw its source, supersede its source, or publish unrelated guidance. The first three read back null; the fourth retains an opaque answer whose shared presentation must be historical/as-of.

Successful corpus receipt fixtures use the accepted test-only wire-result normalizer, exact `[0,20)` quote `Request leave early.`, real seeded source/publication/readership and genuine self authority. They do not introduce a production opaque constructor, a fake PDF extractor, principal overrides or a live provider call. The sequence assertions exercise actual per-call roles and committed state rather than patching receipts or pretending invalid setup is an expected business error.

JavaScript syntax and an explicitly limited ordinary-syntax Can projection are checked. The current prototype does not support typed delivery associations, the accepted corpus declaration, shared-state sequences or page polling. No semantic checker, BDD engine, index/model/provider flow, renderer, reconnect race or browser screenshot was executed. [Verification evidence](../design/jev/complex-knowledge-20261004/verification.md) records the exact checks, exclusions and hashes. The three JEV responses and their material disagreements are preserved in the design; they informed the decision but did not prove correctness.
