# CanDecide

CanDecide helps an operations team review a process change, such as reducing purchase-request delays without removing financial approval. It demonstrates an LLM generating both a decision state and candidate choices, followed by one JEV evaluation containing fixed NOUL/score questions and those reviewed runtime choices. It records a human decision; it never performs the proposed change.

## Evidence and authority

An analyst opens a case with a title, objective and retained source notes. Those inputs and the case owner are immutable; changed underlying evidence needs a new case, not a rewrite beneath old judgments. Source notes remain attributed user-supplied evidence, not independently verified facts. The owner and current team reviewers can read the case and its children. Other analysts receive no implicit access. Only the owning analyst changes activation or requests synthesis; an independent reviewer freezes a brief, and only that current reviewing account can evaluate or decide it. Current role/membership checks apply equally to browser and MCP calls and queued dispatch.

## Generate, review, evaluate, decide

1. The typed synthesizer receives the bounded source notes and fixed company instructions. It produces a derived state distinguishing observations, assumptions and gaps, plus zero to eight concrete alternatives. No supported candidate is a valid result; the model must not invent one to fill a minimum. The declaration-derived candidate-array type validates IDs, uniqueness, collisions with fixed choices and bounds before accepting the result. Schema conformance does not prove factual accuracy.
2. Staff compare the generation with original evidence. An independent reviewer may correct the state and candidates and must explain the review. This freezes the original synthesis, corrected state/options, reviewer and exact normalized judgment specification as business evidence. It sends no JEV request automatically.
3. Before sending, an explicit evaluation requires the current normalized specification to match the approved specification. It sends the objective, retained source notes, reviewed state and corrections with the exact option set in one request. NOUL assesses evidence adequacy, score assesses the whole brief's decision readiness, and choice compares reviewed alternatives plus the company-authored `none` and `need_more_info` exits. Questions are independent: score does not score the candidate selected by choice. Per-candidate scores are outside this workflow.
4. The full model identity, usage, specification revision, NOUL probability, score distribution and choice distribution remain available for review. Unknown IDs, missing/extra probabilities, inconsistent rubric entries or mismatched frozen specifications fail typed normalization; invalid output never becomes a confident fallback choice.
5. A reviewer explicitly records an allowed human choice and reason, including disagreement with the advisory winner. Its normalized specification revision must match the approved review. At that moment the complete valid judgment is captured with the human decision, actor and time as immutable business evidence. No confidence threshold authorizes execution. A recorded decision cannot be rewritten.

Regeneration selects a new draft; older reviews cannot evaluate or decide that new state. A later review similarly supersedes earlier reviews without changing their evidence. New decisions require an available normalized result. There is no pure callback copying every provider result into app state: derived fields observe associated receipts; business captures happen at actual review/decision boundaries. Expired or withheld receipt content stays unavailable and is never synthesized or resent merely to fill history. Already captured evidence has its own ordinary record lifetime and current grants, not an invented infinite receipt guarantee.

## Bounds and recovery

Each case admits at most four synthesis attempts, eight immutable reviews and eight judgment requests. Requests count when durably queued, including failure/unknown outcomes; there is no unproven refund or invisible replacement. Synthesis caps input at 32,768 tokens and output at 3,000; the binding must enforce these limits or reject. Text/candidate limits reject excess rather than truncate. These are resource caps, not currency prices or an aggregate company budget.

Stop suppresses new use and undispatched work for a draft. It does not claim to cancel accepted provider inference; a late response remains attached to its original request and cannot become the current review. A new explicit draft or corrected review can recover within remaining limits. Deactivation blocks generation/evaluation/decision while retaining history; the owner may reactivate the case. Provider failure, unknown acceptance, unavailable result, stale versions and revoked authority remain separate failures under shared contracts.

## UI and inline evidence

The shared server-rendered page shows source notes, generated text, reviewed corrections, exact option sets, specification, full results, human decisions and safe receipt errors. Canonical forms/actions provide generation, review, explicit evaluation, decision and stop; English/Dutch captions describe these actions. Two-second polling reads stored state and never sends inference. No uploaded code, provider keys, browser model calls or custom business renderer is introduced.

Inline tables cover generation bounds/roles, independent review, empty candidates, evaluation limits, current activation, valid differently normalized specification mismatches and valid/foreign human choices. Connected sequences perform real review→pending evaluation→denied decision→stop, an empty-candidate evaluation retaining both escapes, a human disagreement decision from a valid completed associated receipt, immutable decision rejection, and regeneration invalidating an older review. Receipt fixtures are isolated normalized snapshots, not proof that a provider ran or that a sequence advanced a stream.

The handwritten JavaScript is desired generated output. Its shared runtime dependencies, generated runtime-choice schemas, provider normalization, renderer and BDD runner remain unimplemented. Invalid duplicate/reserved/overlong candidate IDs, missing probabilities, malformed result revisions, expired content, current-role revocation and concurrent admissions require shared runtime/typechecker acceptance tests. No live TypeSafe or LLM inference is used by app examples. Design consultations are separate advisory evidence in [the design note](../design/complex-apps/decide.md).
